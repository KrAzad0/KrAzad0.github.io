A1Hermitian conjugates of the ladder operators
Original question

Find the Hermitian conjugate of \(\hat a_\pm\).

Detailed worked solution

Step 1: Define the operators. For the one-dimensional harmonic oscillator,

\[\hat a_- = \sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\left(\hat x+\frac{\iu}{m\omega}\hat p\right),\qquad \hat a_+ = \sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\left(\hat x-\frac{\iu}{m\omega}\hat p\right).\]

The position and momentum observables are self-adjoint: \(\hat x^\dagger=\hat x\), \(\hat p^\dagger=\hat p\). The coefficient \(\sqrt{m\omega/(2\hbar)}\) is real.

Step 2: Take the adjoint term by term. Using \((cA)^\dagger=c^*A^\dagger\), \((A+B)^\dagger=A^\dagger+B^\dagger\), and \(\iu^*=-\iu\),

\[\begin{aligned}\hat a_-^\dagger &=\sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\left(\hat x^\dagger-\frac{\iu}{m\omega}\hat p^\dagger\right)\\ &=\sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\left(\hat x-\frac{\iu\hat p}{m\omega}\right)=\hat a_+.\end{aligned}\]

Similarly,

\[\hat a_+^\dagger=\sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\left(\hat x+\frac{\iu\hat p}{m\omega}\right)=\hat a_-.\]
\[\boxed{\hat a_-^\dagger=\hat a_+,\qquad\hat a_+^\dagger=\hat a_-.}\]

Check in position space. Since \(\hat p=-\iu\hbar\partial_x\), the lowering operator becomes \(\hat a_-=\sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\left(x+\frac{\hbar}{m\omega}\partial_x\right)\). On square-integrable functions decaying so that integration-by-parts surface terms vanish, \((\partial_x)^\dagger=-\partial_x\); hence its formal adjoint is the raising operator. These formulas are adjoint relations on the appropriate oscillator domains, not statements that each operator is individually Hermitian.

Exam-ready conclusion. The ladder operators are mutual Hermitian adjoints, not individually Hermitian: \(\hat a_+\neq \hat a_+^\dagger\) and \(\hat a_-\neq\hat a_-^\dagger\) in general.

A2Canonical ladder commutator
Original question

Show that \([\hat a_-,\hat a_+]=1\).

Detailed worked solution

Step 1: Introduce a convenient coefficient. Let \(c=\sqrt{m\omega/(2\hbar)}\) and \(d=1/(m\omega)\). Then

\[\hat a_-=c(\hat x+\iu d\hat p),\qquad \hat a_+=c(\hat x-\iu d\hat p).\]

Step 2: Expand the commutator.

\[\begin{aligned}[\hat a_-,\hat a_+] &=c^2[\hat x+\iu d\hat p,\hat x-\iu d\hat p]\\ &=c^2\left(-\iu d[\hat x,\hat p]+\iu d[\hat p,\hat x]\right).\end{aligned}\]

The terms \([\hat x,\hat x]\) and \([\hat p,\hat p]\) vanish. Use \([\hat x,\hat p]=\iu\hbar\id\) and \([\hat p,\hat x]=-\iu\hbar\id\):

\[\begin{aligned}[\hat a_-,\hat a_+] &=c^2\left[-\iu d(\iu\hbar)+\iu d(-\iu\hbar)\right]\id\\ &=2c^2d\hbar\id =2\left(\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\right)\frac{\hbar}{m\omega}\id=\id.\end{aligned}\]
\[\boxed{[\hat a_-,\hat a_+]=\hat I.}\]

Equivalently \(\hat a_-\hat a_+=\hat a_+\hat a_-+\hat I\). This noncommutativity is ultimately inherited from \([\hat x,\hat p]=\iu\hbar\hat I\).

A3Why creation raises and annihilation lowers energy
Original question

Explain why \(\hat a_+\) and \(\hat a_-\) are called raising and lowering operators respectively.

Detailed worked solution

Step 1: Define the number operator. Set \(\hat N=\hat a_+\hat a_-\). The commutator identity \([AB,C]=A[B,C]+[A,C]B\) gives

\[\begin{aligned}[\hat N,\hat a_-]&=\hat a_+[\hat a_-,\hat a_-]+[\hat a_+,\hat a_-]\hat a_-=-\hat a_-,\\ [\hat N,\hat a_+]&=\hat a_+[\hat a_-,\hat a_+]+[\hat a_+,\hat a_+]\hat a_-=\hat a_+.\end{aligned}\]

Step 2: Act on a number eigenstate. If \(\hat N\ket n=n\ket n\), then

\[\begin{aligned}\hat N(\hat a_+\ket n)&=(\hat a_+\hat N+[\hat N,\hat a_+])\ket n=(n+1)\hat a_+\ket n,\\ \hat N(\hat a_-\ket n)&=(\hat a_-\hat N+[\hat N,\hat a_-])\ket n=(n-1)\hat a_-\ket n.\end{aligned}\]

So \(\hat a_+\ket n\) lies in the \(n+1\) sector and \(\hat a_-\ket n\) in the \(n-1\) sector (unless the latter vanishes at \(n=0\)).

Step 3: Determine the coefficients by normalization. Write \(\hat a_-\ket n=c_n\ket{n-1}\). Taking the squared norm,

\[|c_n|^2=\bra n\hat a_+\hat a_-\ket n=\bra n\hat N\ket n=n.\]

Choosing the standard real positive phase convention gives \(c_n=\sqrt n\). Similarly,

\[\norm{\hat a_+\ket n}^2=\bra n\hat a_-\hat a_+\ket n=\bra n(\hat N+\id)\ket n=n+1.\]

Thus,

\[\boxed{\hat a_+\ket n=\sqrt{n+1}\ket{n+1},\qquad\hat a_-\ket n=\sqrt n\ket{n-1}.}\]

The energy is \(E_n=\hbar\omega(n+1/2)\), so the energy steps are \(E_{n+1}-E_n=\hbar\omega\). The lowering operator terminates at \(\hat a_-\ket0=0\).

A4Harmonic oscillator Hamiltonian in terms of the number operator
Original question

Express the Hamiltonian of a one-dimensional simple harmonic oscillator in terms of the number operator.

Detailed worked solution

The harmonic oscillator Hamiltonian is \(\hat H=\hat p^2/(2m)+m\omega^2\hat x^2/2\). From the operator definitions,

\[\begin{aligned}\hat a_+\hat a_- &=\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\left(\hat x-\frac{\iu\hat p}{m\omega}\right)\left(\hat x+\frac{\iu\hat p}{m\omega}\right)\\ &=\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\left[\hat x^2+\frac{\hat p^2}{m^2\omega^2}+\frac{\iu}{m\omega}(\hat x\hat p-\hat p\hat x)\right].\end{aligned}\]

Using \([\hat x,\hat p]=\iu\hbar\hat I\),

\[\begin{aligned}\hat N=\hat a_+\hat a_-&=\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\hat x^2+\frac{\hat p^2}{2m\hbar\omega}-\frac12\hat I\\ &=\frac{\hat H}{\hbar\omega}-\frac12\hat I.\end{aligned}\]

Rearrange:

\[\boxed{\hat H=\hbar\omega\left(\hat N+\frac12\hat I\right),\qquad\hat N=\hat a_+\hat a_-.}\]

Because \(\hat N\ket n=n\ket n\), the exact energies are \(E_n=\hbar\omega(n+1/2)\), with ground energy \(\hbar\omega/2\).

A5Hermiticity and self-adjointness of the number operator
Original question

Show that the number operator \(\hat N\) is Hermitian.

Detailed worked solution

Using \(\hat N=\hat a_+\hat a_-\) and \((AB)^\dagger=B^\dagger A^\dagger\),

\[\begin{aligned}\hat N^\dagger&=(\hat a_+\hat a_-)^\dagger=\hat a_-^\dagger\hat a_+^\dagger=\hat a_+\hat a_-=\hat N.\end{aligned}\]
\[\boxed{\hat N^\dagger=\hat N.}\]

Furthermore, for states in the natural domain,

\[\bra\psi\hat N\ket\psi=\bra\psi\hat a_-^\dagger\hat a_-\ket\psi=\norm{\hat a_-\ket\psi}^2\ge0,\]

so \(\hat N\) is positive semidefinite.

Operator-domain qualification. The bare formal identity does not by itself prove equality of full adjoint domains for arbitrary products of unbounded operators. For the oscillator, the number operator is rigorously self-adjoint in the Fock basis on

\[D(\hat N)=\left\{\psi=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_n\ket n:\ \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n^2|c_n|^2<\infty\right\},\]

with \(\hat N\psi=\sum_nnc_n\ket n\). Its spectrum is \(0,1,2,\ldots\), consistent with the operator's nonnegativity.

A6Asymptotic form of the oscillator energy eigenfunctions
Original question

Derive the asymptotic form of the simple harmonic oscillator (SHO) energy eigenfunctions.

Detailed worked solution

Step 1: Write the stationary equation. With \(V(x)=m\omega^2x^2/2\),

\[-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\psi''(x)+\frac12m\omega^2x^2\psi(x)=E\psi(x).\]

Introduce the dimensionless coordinate \(\xi=\sqrt{m\omega/\hbar}\,x\) and \(\epsilon=E/(\hbar\omega)\). The equation becomes

\[\frac{\dd^2\psi}{\dd\xi^2}+(2\epsilon-\xi^2)\psi=0.\]

Step 2: Determine the dominant large-\(|\xi|\) behavior. For \(|\xi|\gg1\), the \(-\xi^2\) term dominates, and the leading equation is \(\psi''-\xi^2\psi\simeq0\). Its exponential leading terms are \(\psi\sim\exp(\pm\xi^2/2)\). Square-integrability excludes the growing branch \(\exp(+\xi^2/2)\), leaving

\[\psi(\xi)\sim \ee^{-\xi^2/2}\times\text{polynomial/algebraic factor}.\]

Step 3: Obtain the polynomial factor. Put \(\psi(\xi)=h(\xi)\ee^{-\xi^2/2}\). Differentiating twice,

\[\psi''=\ee^{-\xi^2/2}\left(h''-2\xi h'+(\xi^2-1)h\right).\]

Substitution yields

\[h''-2\xi h'+(2\epsilon-1)h=0.\]

For bound-state solutions, termination of the appropriate power series produces a Hermite polynomial \(H_n(\xi)\), where \(2\epsilon-1=2n\) and \(\epsilon=n+1/2\), \(n\in\mathbb N_0\).

The exact normalized eigenfunctions are

\[\psi_n(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^nn!}}\left(\frac{m\omega}{\pi\hbar}\right)^{1/4} H_n\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{\hbar}}x\right)\ee^{-m\omega x^2/(2\hbar)}.\]

Since \(H_n(\xi)=2^n\xi^n+O(\xi^{n-2})\) as \(|\xi|\to\infty\) for \(n\ge2\), the leading tail is

\[\boxed{\psi_n(x)\sim C_nx^n\exp\!\left(-\frac{m\omega x^2}{2\hbar}\right),\quad |x|\to\infty.}\]

Here \(C_n\neq0\) is the appropriate \(n\)-dependent constant. The Gaussian suppression dominates the power \(x^n\), so the eigenfunctions are square-integrable.

A7Equality of expectation values in Schr&quot;odinger and Heisenberg pictures
Original question

Show that the expectation value of an observable is the same in both Schr&quot;odinger and Heisenberg pictures of time evolution: \(\langle\hat A\rangle_S=\langle\hat A\rangle_H\).

Detailed worked solution

In the Schr&quot;odinger picture, the state is \(\ket{\psi_S(t)}=\hat U(t,t_0)\ket{\psi_S(t_0)}\). In the Heisenberg picture, define the fixed state \(\ket{\psi_H}=\ket{\psi_S(t_0)}\) and the evolved observable

\[\hat A_H(t)=\hat U^\dagger(t,t_0)\hat A_S(t)\hat U(t,t_0).\]

The Schr&quot;odinger expectation value is

\[\begin{aligned}\avg{\hat A}_S(t)&=\bra{\psi_S(t)}\hat A_S(t)\ket{\psi_S(t)}\\ &=\bra{\psi_H}\hat U^\dagger\hat A_S(t)\hat U\ket{\psi_H}\\ &=\bra{\psi_H}\hat A_H(t)\ket{\psi_H}=\avg{\hat A}_H(t).\end{aligned}\]
\[\boxed{\avg{\hat A}_S(t)=\avg{\hat A}_H(t).}\]

This is an exact unitary equivalence, including the case of an explicitly time-dependent observable. If \(\hat P_S\) is a spectral projector for a measurement event, its corresponding \(\hat P_H=\hat U^\dagger\hat P_S\hat U\) gives the same probability, proving equality of the complete measurement statistics.

Example. For a harmonic oscillator with \(\tau=t-t_0\), \(\hat x_H(t)=\hat x\cos\omega\tau+\hat p\sin\omega\tau/(m\omega)\). Thus, in the fixed initial state,

\[\avg{x}_H(t)=x_0\cos(\omega\tau)+\frac{p_0}{m\omega}\sin(\omega\tau),\]

which agrees with the Schr&quot;odinger result obtained from \(\hat U\ket{\psi(t_0)}\).

A8State and prove Ehrenfest's theorem
Original question

State Ehrenfest's theorem.

Detailed worked solution

Statement. Let \(\hat A_S(t)\) be an observable for which all relevant derivatives and expectations exist. Then

\[\boxed{\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\avg{\hat A}=\frac{\iu}{\hbar}\avg{[\hat H,\hat A]}+\avg{\frac{\partial\hat A}{\partial t}}.}\]

Proof. Start with \(\avg{\hat A}=\bra\psi\hat A\ket\psi\). Differentiating the bra, observable and ket,

\[\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\avg{\hat A}=\bra{\dot\psi}\hat A\ket\psi+\bra\psi\partial_t\hat A\ket\psi+\bra\psi\hat A\ket{\dot\psi}.\]

The Schr&quot;odinger equation \(\iu\hbar\ket{\dot\psi}=\hat H\ket\psi\) and its adjoint give \(\ket{\dot\psi}=-\iu\hat H\ket\psi/\hbar\) and \(\bra{\dot\psi}=+\iu\bra\psi\hat H/\hbar\). Hence

\[\begin{aligned}\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\avg{\hat A} &=\frac{\iu}{\hbar}\bra\psi\hat H\hat A\ket\psi-\frac{\iu}{\hbar}\bra\psi\hat A\hat H\ket\psi+\avg{\partial_t\hat A}\\ &=\frac{\iu}{\hbar}\avg{[\hat H,\hat A]}+\avg{\partial_t\hat A}.\end{aligned}\]

Position example. For \(\hat H=\hat p^2/(2m)+V(\hat x,t)\), the identities \([\hat p^2,\hat x]=-2\iu\hbar\hat p\) and \([V(\hat x,t),\hat p]=\iu\hbar V'(\hat x,t)\) yield

\[\boxed{\frac{\dd\avg{x}}{\dd t}=\frac{\avg p}{m},\qquad\frac{\dd\avg p}{\dd t}=-\avg{V'(\hat x,t)}.}\]

Thus \(m\ddot{\avg x}=-\avg{V'(\hat x,t)}\). For the quadratic oscillator \(V=m\omega^2x^2/2\), the mean follows \(\ddot{\avg x}+\omega^2\avg x=0\) exactly. For a nonlinear \(V\), one cannot generally replace \(\avg{V'(\hat x)}\) by \(V'(\avg x)\).

A9Time-evolution operator, unitarity, and the exponential formula
Original question

Define time evolution operator. Show that it is unitary. Obtain its explicit form for a constant Hamiltonian.

Detailed worked solution

Solve Assignment III Part A Problem 9 rigorously from scratch: define the quantum time-evolution operator \(U(t,t_0)\), derive its differential equation from the Schr&quot;odinger equation, prove unitarity and composition properties, obtain \(U=\exp[-iH(t-t_0)/\hbar]\) for a time-independent self-adjoint Hamiltonian, and provide an examination-ready solution.

Step 1: Definition and initial condition. A closed-system propagator maps an initial state to its evolved state:

\[\ket{\psi(t)}=\hat U(t,t_0)\ket{\psi(t_0)},\qquad\hat U(t_0,t_0)=\hat I.\]

Its composition law is

\[\hat U(t_2,t_1)\hat U(t_1,t_0)=\hat U(t_2,t_0),\qquad\hat U(t,t_0)^{-1}=\hat U(t_0,t).\]

The first identity follows by evolving an arbitrary state through the intermediate time \(t_1\); the second follows by choosing the intermediate evolution to return to the initial time.

Step 2: Differential equation. The time-dependent Schr&quot;odinger equation \(\iu\hbar\partial_t\ket{\psi(t)}=\hat H(t)\ket{\psi(t)}\) becomes, after substitution and cancellation of an arbitrary initial ket,

\[\boxed{\iu\hbar\partial_t\hat U(t,t_0)=\hat H(t)\hat U(t,t_0).}\]

Thus \(\partial_t\hat U=-\iu\hat H(t)\hat U/\hbar\).

Step 3: Unitarity. Taking the adjoint for self-adjoint \(\hat H(t)\) gives \(\partial_t\hat U^\dagger=+\iu\hat U^\dagger\hat H/\hbar\). Therefore,

\[\begin{aligned}\partial_t(\hat U^\dagger\hat U) &=(\partial_t\hat U^\dagger)\hat U+\hat U^\dagger\partial_t\hat U\\ &=\frac{\iu}{\hbar}\hat U^\dagger\hat H\hat U-\frac{\iu}{\hbar}\hat U^\dagger\hat H\hat U=0.\end{aligned}\]

By the initial condition, \(\hat U^\dagger\hat U=\hat I\). The reversible two-sided propagator supplies \(\hat U^{-1}=\hat U^\dagger\), hence \(\hat U\hat U^\dagger=\hat I\) as well. In infinite-dimensional spaces, the first identity alone would show only isometry; the inverse supplies surjectivity.

\[\boxed{\hat U^\dagger\hat U=\hat U\hat U^\dagger=\hat I.}\]

Step 4: Constant Hamiltonian. If \(\hat H(t)=\hat H\), the linear operator ODE with \(\hat U(t_0,t_0)=\hat I\) has the solution

\[\boxed{\hat U(t,t_0)=\exp\!\left[-\frac{\iu\hat H(t-t_0)}{\hbar}\right].}\]

For a bounded \(\hat H\) one checks by differentiating its power series. For an unbounded self-adjoint \(\hat H\), the rigorous definition uses spectral calculus:

\[\hat U(t,t_0)=\int_{\R}\ee^{-\iu E(t-t_0)/\hbar}\,\dd\hat P(E).\]

Every spectral phase has modulus one, proving full unitarity. For a discrete complete energy eigenbasis, \(\hat U=\sum_n\ee^{-\iu E_n\tau/\hbar}\ket n\bra n\), and \(\ket{n,t}=\ee^{-\iu E_n\tau/\hbar}\ket n\).

Time-dependent qualification. In general \(\hat U=\mathcal T\exp[-\frac{\iu}{\hbar}\int_{t_0}^t\hat H(s)\dd s]\); the plain exponential requires commuting Hamiltonians at different times.

A10Bloch's theorem for a one-dimensional periodic potential
Original question

State and prove Bloch's theorem.

Detailed worked solution

Solve Assignment III Part A Problem 10 rigorously from scratch at M.Sc. mathematical physics level: state and prove Bloch's theorem for a periodic one-dimensional potential, establish that the lattice translation operator commutes with the Hamiltonian, derive \(\psi_k(x)=e^{ikx}u_k(x)\) with periodic \(u_k\), explain crystal momentum, reciprocal lattice and Brillouin zones, derive the Bloch boundary condition, and provide a detailed exam-ready proof.

Theorem. Let \(\hat H=-\hbar^2\partial_x^2/(2m)+V(x)\) with real periodic \(V(x+a)=V(x)\), \(a>0\). Its generalized energy eigenfunctions can be chosen in the Bloch form

\[\boxed{\psi_{nk}(x)=\ee^{\iu kx}u_{nk}(x),\qquad u_{nk}(x+a)=u_{nk}(x).}\]

Step 1: Translation symmetry. Define \((\hat T_a\psi)(x)=\psi(x+a)\). This is unitary on \(L^2(\R)\), since substituting \(y=x+a\) preserves \(\int|\psi|^2\dd x\), and \(\hat T_a^{-1}=\hat T_{-a}\). For a smooth test function,

\[\begin{aligned}(\hat H\hat T_a\psi)(x)&=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\psi''(x+a)+V(x)\psi(x+a),\\ (\hat T_a\hat H\psi)(x)&=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\psi''(x+a)+V(x+a)\psi(x+a).\end{aligned}\]

By periodicity, the expressions coincide and \(\boxed{[\hat H,\hat T_a]=0}\).

Step 2: Eigenvalue of translation. For a finite periodic crystal of length \(L=Na\), with Born–von Karman boundary condition \(\psi(x+L)=\psi(x)\), the energy eigenspaces admit simultaneous eigenvectors of the commuting unitary translation. Write

\[\hat T_a\psi_k=\lambda\psi_k,\qquad\psi_k(x+a)=\lambda\psi_k(x).\]

Because \(\hat T_a\) is unitary, \(|\lambda|=1\), so \(\lambda=\ee^{\iu ka}\) with real \(k\). The translation equation is

\[\boxed{\psi_k(x+a)=\ee^{\iu ka}\psi_k(x).}\]

Step 3: Periodic factor. Define \(u_k(x)=\ee^{-\iu kx}\psi_k(x)\). Then

\[\begin{aligned}u_k(x+a)&=\ee^{-\iu k(x+a)}\psi_k(x+a)\\ &=\ee^{-\iu kx}\ee^{-\iu ka}\ee^{\iu ka}\psi_k(x)=u_k(x).\end{aligned}\]

Hence \(\psi_k(x)=\ee^{\iu kx}u_k(x)\), with \(u_k(x+a)=u_k(x)\): Bloch's theorem.

Boundary conditions on one cell. For a sufficiently regular potential, the quasiperiodic boundary conditions are

\[\boxed{\psi_k(a)=\ee^{\iu ka}\psi_k(0),\qquad \psi_k'(a)=\ee^{\iu ka}\psi_k'(0).}\]

For \(u_k\), these become ordinary periodic conditions \(u_k(a)=u_k(0)\) and \(u_k'(a)=u_k'(0)\).

Crystal momentum. Since \(\ee^{\iu(k+G)a}=\ee^{\iu ka}\) for reciprocal vectors \(G=2\pi j/a\), \(j\in\mathbb Z\), \(k\) is defined modulo \(G\). The quantity \(\hbar k\) is crystal momentum (quasimomentum), generally distinct from mechanical momentum \(\hat p\). A convenient first Brillouin zone is \(-\pi/a\le k<\pi/a\). For \(N\) periodic cells, \(\hat T_a^N=\hat I\) implies \(\ee^{\iu kNa}=1\) and

\[\boxed{k_j=\frac{2\pi j}{Na};\qquad N\text{ distinct values per Brillouin zone}.}\]

Unit-cell equation. Substituting \(\psi_k=\ee^{\iu kx}u_k\) into the stationary Schr&quot;odinger equation produces

\[\boxed{\left[\frac{(-\iu\hbar\partial_x+\hbar k)^2}{2m}+V(x)\right]u_{nk}(x)=E_n(k)u_{nk}(x).}\]

The resulting band functions satisfy \(E_n(k+G)=E_n(k)\) with consistent band labeling. Infinite-crystal Bloch waves are generalized eigenfunctions; the complete rigorous framework is Bloch–Floquet direct-integral theory.

B1Ground-state energy and eigenfunction by the algebraic method
Original question

Derive the ground-state energy eigenfunction (up to the normalization constant) and eigenvalue for a SHO in 1D using algebraic method.

Detailed worked solution

Solve Assignment III Part B Problem 1 rigorously from scratch at M.Sc. mathematical physics level: derive the ground-state energy eigenfunction (up to normalization) and eigenvalue of the one-dimensional quantum simple harmonic oscillator using the algebraic ladder-operator method, establish \(a_-|0\rangle=0\), solve the resulting differential equation, explain zero-point energy, and give a detailed 15-mark exam-ready solution.

Step 1: Factorize the Hamiltonian. The oscillator Hamiltonian is

\[\hat H=\frac{\hat p^2}{2m}+\frac12m\omega^2\hat x^2.\]

The lowering and raising operators are

\[\hat a_-=\sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\left(\hat x+\frac{\iu\hat p}{m\omega}\right),\quad \hat a_+=\sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\left(\hat x-\frac{\iu\hat p}{m\omega}\right).\]

Using \([\hat x,\hat p]=\iu\hbar\id\), their product is

\[\begin{aligned}\hat a_+\hat a_-&=\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\left[\hat x^2+\frac{\hat p^2}{m^2\omega^2}+\frac{\iu}{m\omega}[\hat x,\hat p]\right]\\ &=\frac{\hat H}{\hbar\omega}-\frac12\hat I.\end{aligned}\]

Thus,

\[\boxed{\hat H=\hbar\omega\left(\hat a_-^\dagger\hat a_-+\frac12\hat I\right).}\]

Step 2: Prove the energy lower bound. For every normalized state in the form domain,

\[\begin{aligned}\avg{H}_\psi&=\hbar\omega\bra\psi\hat a_-^\dagger\hat a_-\ket\psi+\frac12\hbar\omega\\ &=\hbar\omega\norm{\hat a_-\ket\psi}^2+\frac12\hbar\omega\ge\frac12\hbar\omega.\end{aligned}\]

Equality requires \(\norm{\hat a_-\ket\psi}=0\), so the energy-minimizing state \(\ket0\) must obey

\[\boxed{\hat a_-\ket0=0.}\]

A square-integrable solution will establish that this bound is attained, hence \(E_0=\hbar\omega/2\).

Step 3: Convert the ground-state condition into an ODE. In the position representation \(\hat p=-\iu\hbar\partial_x\), so

\[\begin{aligned}\hat a_-\psi_0(x)&=\sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\left(x+\frac{\hbar}{m\omega}\frac{\dd}{\dd x}\right)\psi_0(x)=0.\end{aligned}\]

Cancel the nonzero prefactor:

\[\boxed{\frac{\dd\psi_0}{\dd x}=-\frac{m\omega}{\hbar}x\psi_0.}\]

Separate variables and integrate:

\[\begin{aligned}\frac{\dd\psi_0}{\psi_0}&=-\frac{m\omega}{\hbar}x\,\dd x,\\ \ln\psi_0&=-\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}x^2+C.\end{aligned}\]

Exponentiate and absorb \(\ee^C\) into \(A_0\):

\[\boxed{\psi_0(x)=A_0\exp\!\left(-\frac{m\omega x^2}{2\hbar}\right),\qquad E_0=\frac12\hbar\omega.}\]

The Gaussian is square integrable and has no nodes; as the first-order ODE determines its solution up to a scalar, the ground state is nondegenerate.

Step 4: Normalize. Imposing \(1=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\psi_0(x)|^2\,\dd x\) and the Gaussian integral \(\int\ee^{-\alpha x^2}\dd x=\sqrt{\pi/\alpha}\) on \(\R\),

\[1=|A_0|^2\sqrt{\frac{\pi\hbar}{m\omega}},\quad \boxed{A_0=\left(\frac{m\omega}{\pi\hbar}\right)^{1/4}}\]

with overall phase chosen zero.

Step 5: Verify in the original Schr&quot;odinger equation. Write \(\beta=m\omega/\hbar\). Then \(\psi'_0=-\beta x\psi_0\) and \(\psi''_0=(\beta^2x^2-\beta)\psi_0\). Hence

\[\begin{aligned}\hat H\psi_0&=\left[-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}(\beta^2x^2-\beta)+\frac12m\omega^2x^2\right]\psi_0\\ &=\frac12\hbar\omega\psi_0,\end{aligned}\]

confirming the eigenvalue.

Zero-point energy. The uncertainty relation prevents simultaneous sharp position and momentum at the classical equilibrium point. The ket and bra equations are

\[\ket{\dot\psi}=-\frac{\iu}{\hbar}\hat H\ket\psi,\qquad\bra{\dot\psi}=\frac{\iu}{\hbar}\bra\psi\hat H.\]

The opposite signs come from complex conjugation of \(\iu\) and self-adjointness of \(\hat H\).

\[\begin{aligned}\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\avg{\hat A}&=\bra{\dot\psi}\hat A\ket\psi+\bra\psi\frac{\partial\hat A}{\partial t}\ket\psi+\bra\psi\hat A\ket{\dot\psi}\\ &=\frac{\iu}{\hbar}\bra\psi\hat H\hat A\ket\psi+\avg{\partial_t\hat A}-\frac{\iu}{\hbar}\bra\psi\hat A\hat H\ket\psi\\ &=\frac{\iu}{\hbar}\avg{\hat H\hat A-\hat A\hat H}+\avg{\partial_t\hat A}.\end{aligned}\]

\[\boxed{\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\avg{\hat A}=\frac{\iu}{\hbar}\avg{[\hat H,\hat A]}+\avg{\frac{\partial\hat A}{\partial t}}.}\]

The first term is the change caused by the state dynamics; the second is the explicit operator time dependence. Omitting the second term is incorrect whenever \(\hat A_S(t)\) is externally time dependent.

\[\boxed{\frac{\dd\avg x}{\dd t}=\frac{\avg p}{m},\qquad\frac{\dd\avg p}{\dd t}=-\avg{\partial_xV}.}\]

For the oscillator \(V=m\omega^2x^2/2\), one obtains the exact mean equation \(\ddot{\avg x}+\omega^2\avg x=0\).

Step 5: Explicitly time-dependent example. Let \(\hat A(t)=f(t)\hat x\) with real differentiable \(f\). Then \(\partial_t\hat A=f'(t)\hat x\), while \([\hat H,f\hat x]=f[\hat H,\hat x]\). Hence

Boundary and domain terms must vanish or be accounted for.

B7The Schr&quot;odinger picture of time evolution
Original question

Definition. In the Schr&quot;odinger picture, the physical state carries dynamical time dependence. Observable operators are fixed unless they depend explicitly on time. The normalized state \(\ket{\psi_S(t)}\in\Hil\) obeys the fundamental Schr&quot;odinger dynamical postulate,

\[\boxed{\iu\hbar\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\ket{\psi_S(t)}=\hat H_S(t)\ket{\psi_S(t)}.}\]

Derivation from an infinitesimal propagator. Continuous unitary dynamics with self-adjoint generator \(\hat H_S(t)\) has, on suitable states,

\[\hat U(t+\dd t,t)=\hat I-\frac{\iu}{\hbar}\hat H_S(t)\dd t+o(\dd t).\]

Then \(\ket{\psi_S(t+\dd t)}=\hat U(t+\dd t,t)\ket{\psi_S(t)}\); subtracting \(\ket{\psi_S(t)}\), dividing by \(\dd t\), and taking the limit recovers the Schr&quot;odinger equation. This is a derivation conditional on choosing the Hamiltonian as the generator; the dynamical law itself is a quantum postulate, not deduced from Hilbert-space axioms alone.

Time evolution. Define

\[\boxed{\ket{\psi_S(t)}=\hat U(t,t_0)\ket{\psi_S(t_0)},\qquad\hat U(t_0,t_0)=\hat I.}\]

Differentiating for arbitrary initial states yields

\[\boxed{\iu\hbar\partial_t\hat U(t,t_0)=\hat H_S(t)\hat U(t,t_0).}\]

For a time-independent self-adjoint Hamiltonian,

\[\boxed{\hat U(t,t_0)=\exp\!\left[-\frac{\iu\hat H(t-t_0)}{\hbar}\right].}\]

For general time dependence, the solution is the appropriately defined time-ordered exponential.

\[\begin{aligned}\frac{\dd}{\dd t}(\hat U^\dagger\hat U)&=\frac{\iu}{\hbar}\hat U^\dagger\hat H_S\hat U-\frac{\iu}{\hbar}\hat U^\dagger\hat H_S\hat U=0.\end{aligned}\]

The initial condition and reversibility imply \(\hat U^\dagger\hat U=\hat U\hat U^\dagger=\hat I\), preserving \(\norm{\ket\psi}\) and all inner products.

Position-space form. For a single particle with \(\hat H_S(t)=\hat p^2/(2m)+V(\hat x,t)\), projection on the position basis yields

\[\boxed{\iu\hbar\partial_t\psi_S(x,t)=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\partial_x^2\psi_S(x,t)+V(x,t)\psi_S(x,t).}\]

For a real potential, combining this with its conjugate gives the continuity equation

\[\boxed{\partial_t\rho+\partial_xj=0,\quad\rho=|\psi_S|^2,\quad j=\frac{\hbar}{2m\iu}(\psi_S^*\partial_x\psi_S-\psi_S\partial_x\psi_S^*).}\]

Integration across space with vanishing boundary flux proves probability conservation.

Expectation values. For any observable, possibly explicitly time dependent,

\[\boxed{\avg A_S(t)=\bra{\psi_S(t)}\hat A_S(t)\ket{\psi_S(t)}.}\]

Differentiating all three factors and using the Schr&quot;odinger equation yields

\[\boxed{\frac{\dd\avg A}{\dd t}=\frac{\iu}{\hbar}\avg{[\hat H_S,\hat A_S]}+\avg{\partial_t\hat A_S}.}\]

The operator \(\hat x_S=x\) may remain fixed in this picture while \(\avg{x}_S(t)\) varies through the evolving state.

Stationary states. For \(\hat H\ket n=E_n\ket n\),

\[\boxed{\ket{n,t}=\ee^{-\iu E_n\tau/\hbar}\ket n.}\]

This differs from \(\ket n\) by a common phase and has time-independent position probability density \(|\phi_n(x)|^2\). A general discrete energy superposition evolves as

\[\boxed{\ket{\psi_S(t)}=\sum_nc_n\ee^{-\iu E_n\tau/\hbar}\ket n.}\]

Its relative phases \(\exp[-\iu(E_m-E_n)\tau/\hbar]\) can produce oscillatory expectation values:

\[\avg A(t)=\sum_{m,n}c_m^*c_n\ee^{\iu(E_m-E_n)\tau/\hbar}\bra m\hat A\ket n.\]

\[\boxed{\ket{\psi_S(t)}=\frac1{\sqrt2}\left(\ee^{-\iu\omega t/2}\ket0+\ee^{-3\iu\omega t/2}\ket1\right).}\]

With \(\hat x=\sqrt{\hbar/(2m\omega)}(\hat a_-+\hat a_+)\) and \(\hat p=\iu\sqrt{m\hbar\omega/2}(\hat a_+-\hat a_-)\), only the off-diagonal \(0\leftrightarrow1\) matrix elements contribute. The exact results are

\[\boxed{\avg{x}(t)=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}\cos(\omega t),\qquad \avg p(t)=-\sqrt{\frac{m\hbar\omega}{2}}\sin(\omega t).}\]

They satisfy Ehrenfest's equations. The interference changes while the average energy stays constant. The Heisenberg picture reproduces these expectation values exactly.

Explain the Heisenberg picture of time evolution. Derive the equation of motion in Heisenberg's picture.

This transfers the dynamical time dependence from the state to the observable operators.

\[\avg A_S(t)=\bra{\psi_S(t)}\hat A_S(t)\ket{\psi_S(t)}.\]

Inserting \(\ket{\psi_S(t)}=\hat U\ket{\psi_H}\) and its adjoint gives

\[\begin{aligned}\avg A_S(t)&=\bra{\psi_H}\hat U^\dagger\hat A_S(t)\hat U\ket{\psi_H}.\end{aligned}\]

\[\boxed{\hat A_H(t)=\hat U^\dagger(t,t_0)\hat A_S(t)\hat U(t,t_0).}\]

At the reference time \(t_0\), the pictures agree: \(\hat A_H(t_0)=\hat A_S(t_0)\).

\[\hat U^\dagger[\hat H_S,\hat A_S]\hat U=[\hat H_H,\hat A_H].\]

\[\boxed{\frac{\dd\hat A_H}{\dd t}=\frac{\iu}{\hbar}[\hat H_H,\hat A_H]+\left(\frac{\partial\hat A_S}{\partial t}\right)_H.}\]

The derivative of the explicit Schr&quot;odinger operator is not generally the entire Heisenberg derivative. If \(\partial_t\hat A_S=0\), the last term vanishes; if \(\hat H_S\) is also time independent, then \(\hat H_H=\hat H_S=\hat H\).

Step 4: Position and momentum. For \(\hat H=\hat p^2/(2m)+V(\hat x)\),

\[\begin{aligned}\dot{\hat x}_H&=\frac{\iu}{\hbar}[\hat H,\hat x_H]=\frac{\hat p_H}{m},\\ \dot{\hat p}_H&=\frac{\iu}{\hbar}[\hat H,\hat p_H]=-V'(\hat x_H).\end{aligned}\]

The commutators follow from \([\hat p_H^2,\hat x_H]=-2\iu\hbar\hat p_H\) and \([V(\hat x_H),\hat p_H]=\iu\hbar V'(\hat x_H)\). Hence

\[\boxed{m\ddot{\hat x}_H=-V'(\hat x_H).}\]

For a free particle, \(V=0\) gives \(\hat p_H(t)=\hat p\), \(\hat x_H(t)=\hat x+\tau\hat p/m\). For a harmonic oscillator, the solution is sinusoidal and is derived fully in B9.

Step 5: Physical equivalence. The expectation in the Heisenberg picture is

\[\begin{aligned}\avg A_H(t)&=\bra{\psi_H}\hat A_H(t)\ket{\psi_H}\\ &=\bra{\psi_S(t_0)}\hat U^\dagger\hat A_S(t)\hat U\ket{\psi_S(t_0)}\\ &=\bra{\psi_S(t)}\hat A_S(t)\ket{\psi_S(t)}=\avg A_S(t).\end{aligned}\]

Thus the two pictures give identical expectation values and, by transforming spectral projectors, identical measurement probabilities.

Step 6: Constants of motion. If \(\partial_t\hat A_S=0\) and \([\hat H_S(t),\hat A_S]=0\) for all \(t\), then \(\dot{\hat A}_H=0\). For \(\hat A_S(t)=\hat H_S(t)\),

\[\boxed{\dot{\hat H}_H=\hat U^\dagger(\partial_t\hat H_S)\hat U.}\]

In particular, energy is conserved for a time-independent Hamiltonian. For unbounded operators, the equations are interpreted on appropriate common domains or through matrix elements.

B9Exact Heisenberg evolution of position and momentum for a 1D SHO
Original question

Derive the time evolution of position \([\hat x_H(t)]\) and momentum operator \([\hat p_H(t)]\) of a 1D SHO in Heisenberg's picture.

Detailed worked solution

Solve Assignment III Part B Problem 9 rigorously from scratch at M.Sc. mathematical physics level: derive the exact time evolution of the Heisenberg position and momentum operators for a one-dimensional quantum harmonic oscillator, both by solving the coupled Heisenberg equations and by evolving annihilation/creation operators; verify the canonical commutator, derive expectation values, discuss stationary number states and coherent states, and give a complete 15-mark exam-ready solution.

Set-up. The time-independent harmonic oscillator Hamiltonian is

\[\boxed{\hat H=\frac{\hat p^2}{2m}+\frac12m\omega^2\hat x^2,\qquad[\hat x,\hat p]=\iu\hbar\hat I.}\]

Define \(\tau=t-t_0\), \(\hat x_H(t_0)=\hat x\), and \(\hat p_H(t_0)=\hat p\). As \(\hat H\) is time independent, \(\hat H_H=\hat H\); the Heisenberg equation is \(\dot{\hat A}_H=(\iu/\hbar)[\hat H,\hat A_H]\).

Method I: Coupled equations and initial conditions

Step 1: Position commutator. Using the canonical relation,

\[\begin{aligned}[\hat p_H^2,\hat x_H]&=\hat p_H[\hat p_H,\hat x_H]+[\hat p_H,\hat x_H]\hat p_H=-2\iu\hbar\hat p_H,\\ [\hat H,\hat x_H]&=\frac1{2m}[\hat p_H^2,\hat x_H]=-\frac{\iu\hbar}{m}\hat p_H.\end{aligned}\]

Thus

\[\boxed{\dot{\hat x}_H=\frac{\hat p_H}{m}.}\]

Step 2: Momentum commutator. Similarly,

\[\begin{aligned}[\hat x_H^2,\hat p_H]&=\hat x_H[\hat x_H,\hat p_H]+[\hat x_H,\hat p_H]\hat x_H=2\iu\hbar\hat x_H,\\ [\hat H,\hat p_H]&=\frac{m\omega^2}{2}[\hat x_H^2,\hat p_H]=\iu\hbar m\omega^2\hat x_H.\end{aligned}\]

Therefore

\[\boxed{\dot{\hat p}_H=-m\omega^2\hat x_H.}\]

Step 3: Decouple. Differentiating the position equation and inserting the momentum equation,

\[\ddot{\hat x}_H=\frac1m\dot{\hat p}_H=-\omega^2\hat x_H,\qquad\boxed{\ddot{\hat x}_H+\omega^2\hat x_H=0.}\]

Step 4: Solve as an operator-valued ODE. The general solution is

\[\hat x_H(t)=\hat C\cos(\omega\tau)+\hat D\sin(\omega\tau).\]

At \(\tau=0\), \(\hat C=\hat x\). Differentiating, \(\dot{\hat x}_H(t_0)=\omega\hat D\). By \(\dot{\hat x}_H(t_0)=\hat p/m\), we get \(\hat D=\hat p/(m\omega)\). Therefore,

\[\boxed{\hat x_H(t)=\hat x\cos(\omega\tau)+\frac{\hat p}{m\omega}\sin(\omega\tau).}\]

Now apply \(\hat p_H=m\dot{\hat x}_H\):

\[\boxed{\hat p_H(t)=\hat p\cos(\omega\tau)-m\omega\hat x\sin(\omega\tau).}\]

Direct differential check. Differentiating these formulas produces \(\dot{\hat x}_H=\hat p_H/m\) and \(\dot{\hat p}_H=-m\omega^2\hat x_H\), and the equations reduce to \(\hat x\), \(\hat p\) at \(\tau=0\). Thus both the equations and their initial conditions are satisfied.

Method II: Time evolution of the ladder operators

Step 1: Ladder representation. Define

\[\begin{aligned}\hat a_-&=\sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\left(\hat x+\frac{\iu\hat p}{m\omega}\right),\\ \hat a_+&=\sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\left(\hat x-\frac{\iu\hat p}{m\omega}\right).\end{aligned}\]

Then \([\hat a_-,\hat a_+]=\hat I\), \(\hat N=\hat a_+\hat a_-\), and

\[\boxed{\hat H=\hbar\omega\left(\hat N+\frac12\hat I\right).}\]

Step 2: Hamiltonian commutators. Using \([AB,C]=A[B,C]+[A,C]B\),

\[[\hat N,\hat a_-]=-\hat a_-,\qquad[\hat N,\hat a_+]=\hat a_+.\]

Therefore,

\[\boxed{[\hat H,\hat a_-]=-\hbar\omega\hat a_-,\qquad[\hat H,\hat a_+]=+\hbar\omega\hat a_+.}\]

Step 3: Solve their Heisenberg equations. The equations are

\[\frac{\dd\hat a_{-,H}}{\dd t}=-\iu\omega\hat a_{-,H},\qquad\frac{\dd\hat a_{+,H}}{\dd t}=+\iu\omega\hat a_{+,H}.\]

With \(\hat a_{\pm,H}(t_0)=\hat a_\pm\), the solutions are

\[\boxed{\hat a_{-,H}(t)=\hat a_-\ee^{-\iu\omega\tau},\qquad\hat a_{+,H}(t)=\hat a_+\ee^{+\iu\omega\tau}.}\]

The opposite phases preserve the adjoint relation and the ladder commutator.

Step 4: Reconstruct position and momentum. Recall

\[\hat x=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}(\hat a_-+\hat a_+),\quad\hat p=\iu\sqrt{\frac{m\hbar\omega}{2}}(\hat a_+-\hat a_-).\]

Then

\[\begin{aligned}\hat x_H(t)&=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}\left(\hat a_-\ee^{-\iu\omega\tau}+\hat a_+\ee^{\iu\omega\tau}\right),\\ \hat p_H(t)&=\iu\sqrt{\frac{m\hbar\omega}{2}}\left(\hat a_+\ee^{\iu\omega\tau}-\hat a_-\ee^{-\iu\omega\tau}\right).\end{aligned}\]

Using \(\ee^{\pm\iu\theta}=\cos\theta\pm\iu\sin\theta\) and the original expressions for \(\hat x\) and \(\hat p\), these become

\[\boxed{\hat x_H(t)=\hat x\cos\omega\tau+\frac{\hat p}{m\omega}\sin\omega\tau,\quad\hat p_H(t)=\hat p\cos\omega\tau-m\omega\hat x\sin\omega\tau.}\]

The second independent derivation agrees exactly with Method I.

Canonical commutator and conservation of energy

Let \(C=\cos\omega\tau\) and \(S=\sin\omega\tau\). From \(\hat x_H=C\hat x+(S/(m\omega))\hat p\) and \(\hat p_H=C\hat p-m\omega S\hat x\),

\[\begin{aligned}[\hat x_H,\hat p_H] &=C^2[\hat x,\hat p]-S^2[\hat p,\hat x]\\ &=\iu\hbar(C^2+S^2)\hat I=\iu\hbar\hat I.\end{aligned}\]
\[\boxed{[\hat x_H(t),\hat p_H(t)]=\iu\hbar\hat I.}\]

The number operator is also invariant:

\[\hat N_H(t)=\hat a_{+,H}(t)\hat a_{-,H}(t)=\hat a_+\hat a_-\ee^{\iu\omega\tau}\ee^{-\iu\omega\tau}=\hat N.\]

Thus \(\hat H_H=\hbar\omega(\hat N_H+1/2)=\hat H\), showing energy conservation.

Expectation values for an arbitrary initial state

Let \(x_0=\bra{\psi_H}\hat x\ket{\psi_H}\) and \(p_0=\bra{\psi_H}\hat p\ket{\psi_H}\). Taking expectations gives

\[\boxed{\begin{aligned} \avg x(t)&=x_0\cos\omega\tau+\frac{p_0}{m\omega}\sin\omega\tau,\\ \avg p(t)&=p_0\cos\omega\tau-m\omega x_0\sin\omega\tau. \end{aligned}}\]

Differentiation verifies \(\dot{\avg x}=\avg p/m\), \(\dot{\avg p}=-m\omega^2\avg x\), and \(\ddot{\avg x}+\omega^2\avg x=0\). These results are exact for all states with finite first moments, not just near-classical wavepackets.

Stationary number states

For \(\hat N\ket n=n\ket n\), ladder orthogonality gives \(\bra n\hat a_\pm\ket n=0\). Thus the Heisenberg operators have zero mean in a number eigenstate:

\[\boxed{\avg x_n(t)=\avg p_n(t)=0.}\]

This does not mean zero fluctuations. From B2,

\[\boxed{\Delta x_n^2=\frac{\hbar}{m\omega}\left(n+\frac12\right),\qquad\Delta p_n^2=m\hbar\omega\left(n+\frac12\right).}\]

The variances are constant because \(\ket n\) is stationary up to a phase in the Schr&quot;odinger picture. Their product is \(\hbar(n+1/2)\); only \(n=0\) among number states saturates \(\hbar/2\).

Coherent states and classical-like motion

Define a normalized coherent state \(\ket\alpha\), \(\alpha\in\mathbb C\), by

\[\boxed{\hat a_-\ket\alpha=\alpha\ket\alpha,\qquad\ket\alpha=\ee^{-|\alpha|^2/2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^n}{\sqrt{n!}}\ket n.}\]

To verify its eigenvalue equation, act with \(\hat a_-\), use \(\hat a_-\ket n=\sqrt n\ket{n-1}\), and relabel \(r=n-1\); the factor \(\alpha\) emerges from every term. Normalization follows from \(\sum_{n\ge0}|\alpha|^{2n}/n!=\ee^{|\alpha|^2}\).

In the fixed Heisenberg state \(\ket\alpha\), \(\avg{\hat a_-}=\alpha\) and \(\avg{\hat a_+}=\alpha^*\). Consequently,

\[\begin{aligned}\avg x_\alpha(t)&=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}\left(\alpha\ee^{-\iu\omega\tau}+\alpha^*\ee^{\iu\omega\tau}\right)\\ &=\sqrt{\frac{2\hbar}{m\omega}}\operatorname{Re}(\alpha\ee^{-\iu\omega\tau}),\\ \avg p_\alpha(t)&=\iu\sqrt{\frac{m\hbar\omega}{2}}\left(\alpha^*\ee^{\iu\omega\tau}-\alpha\ee^{-\iu\omega\tau}\right)\\ &=\sqrt{2m\hbar\omega}\operatorname{Im}(\alpha\ee^{-\iu\omega\tau}).\end{aligned}\]

Writing \(\alpha=|\alpha|\ee^{\iu\phi}\), with \(A=\sqrt{2\hbar/(m\omega)}|\alpha|\), gives

\[\boxed{\avg x_\alpha(t)=A\cos(\omega\tau-\phi),\qquad\avg p_\alpha(t)=-m\omega A\sin(\omega\tau-\phi).}\]

These mean values follow a classical harmonic orbit. In the Schr&quot;odinger picture the coherent state evolves as

\[\boxed{\hat U(t,t_0)\ket\alpha=\ee^{-\iu\omega\tau/2}\ket{\alpha\ee^{-\iu\omega\tau}}.}\]

Using \(\avg{\hat a_-^2}=\alpha^2\), \(\avg{\hat a_+^2}=(\alpha^*)^2\), \(\avg{\hat a_+\hat a_-}=|\alpha|^2\), and \(\avg{\hat a_-\hat a_+}=|\alpha|^2+1\), one finds

\[\boxed{\Delta x_\alpha^2=\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega},\quad\Delta p_\alpha^2=\frac{m\hbar\omega}{2},\quad\Delta x_\alpha\Delta p_\alpha=\frac\hbar2.}\]

The coherent state remains minimum-uncertainty at all times and has mean energy

\[\boxed{\avg H_\alpha=\hbar\omega\left(|\alpha|^2+\frac12\right).}\]

Symplectic form of the evolution

The exact operator evolution is linear and may be written

\[\boxed{\begin{pmatrix}\hat x_H(t)\\\hat p_H(t)\end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix}\cos\omega\tau&\dfrac{\sin\omega\tau}{m\omega}\\[4pt]-m\omega\sin\omega\tau&\cos\omega\tau\end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix}\hat x\\\hat p\end{pmatrix}.}\]

The matrix has determinant one and satisfies \(M^TJM=J\), where \(J=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\). This symplectic property is the classical counterpart of exact canonical commutator preservation. The simple closed form relies on the quadratic Hamiltonian; general nonlinear potentials do not give the same linear closed system.

Fifteen-mark exam-ready conclusion. From \(\dot{\hat x}_H=(\iu/\hbar)[\hat H,\hat x_H]=\hat p_H/m\) and \(\dot{\hat p}_H=(\iu/\hbar)[\hat H,\hat p_H]=-m\omega^2\hat x_H\), deduce \(\ddot{\hat x}_H+\omega^2\hat x_H=0\). Apply \(\hat x_H(t_0)=\hat x\), \(\hat p_H(t_0)=\hat p\) to obtain the two boxed sinusoidal operators. Alternatively, \([\hat H,\hat a_\mp]=\mp\hbar\omega\hat a_\mp\) gives opposite ladder phases; substituting into \(\hat x\) and \(\hat p\) reproduces both results. The explicit commutator calculation yields \([\hat x_H,\hat p_H]=\iu\hbar\hat I\). This completes the exact derivation.
