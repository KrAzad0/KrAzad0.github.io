About Me

I am an independent researcher based in Bihar, India, with an interdisciplinary background in Physics, Mathematics, and Quantum Technology. Having completed an M.Sc. in Mathematics (Online) from Mangalayatan University and currently pursuing an M.Sc. in Physics (Quantum Technology) at SRM Institute of Science and Technology (SRMIST), I am interested in developing mathematically rigorous approaches to problems at the intersection of theoretical physics and modern computational science.

My research interests include mathematical physics, quantum mechanics, quantum computing, number theory, complex analysis, and special functions. I am particularly interested in investigating identities from Ramanujan’s Lost Notebook and exploring their potential connections to spectral theory, quantum systems, and mathematical structures in field theory.

Another major area of interest is positive geometry and scattering amplitudes, especially the mathematical structures underlying QCD kinematics, twistor-space methods, and amplituhedron formulations. I aim to investigate how geometric, algebraic, and analytic techniques can provide deeper insights into quantum field theory and fundamental interactions.

My broader goal is to develop reproducible, computationally supported research that connects abstract mathematical structures with physical problems, using tools such as Python, symbolic computation, and numerical simulation. I am particularly interested in independent research projects, collaborative opportunities, and research internships in theoretical physics, quantum computing, and mathematical physics.
